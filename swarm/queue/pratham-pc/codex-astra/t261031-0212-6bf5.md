The app judges CPR posture from one phone camera using MediaPipe 2D landmarks (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip on the near arm, plus visibility, and both shoulders for a side-on check). Users saw wrong feedback even with deliberately bent elbows.
In Python outside this folder, model a kneeling rescuer as 3D joints with known true elbow angle and known shoulder-over-wrist offset, projected through a pinhole camera. Sweep camera yaw from 0 (side-on) to 60 degrees, camera height, distance and phone tilt, with landmark noise matching tools/testdata/real-cph2467-*.csv (measure its frame-to-frame jitter). Answer with numbers: how much does the 2D elbow angle err with yaw, and how far can yaw go before bent (say 130 degrees true) can't be told from straight (170)? Same for shoulder-over-wrist. Which side-on measure estimates yaw best from what we have: shoulder gap over torso length, hip gap, or both? Which thresholds separate straight, bent and leaning, and with what false alarm and miss rates at each yaw? How long a median window is needed during compressions? Is MediaPipe's own 3D world-landmark angle likely to help (say what is known, cite sources, and don't invent)?
Then look at real-cph2467-*.csv distributions and say which sessions look consistent with what yaw, labelled as inference. Give concrete recommended constants and the order of checks. Say plainly what only a labelled recording can settle.
