# How to film a real-person test

1. **Prepare the phone.** Before recording, make sure its battery is above 40%, it is not hot, and other apps are closed. Never call 112.
2. **Set up the rescuer.** Use a firm surface. A folded firm cushion on the floor is better than a soft pillow, which can hide the movement. Keep arms straight and locked, shoulders over hands, and push clearly down and all the way back up. Continue for the full two minutes.
3. **Position the holder.** Stand side-on to the rescuer and hold the phone sideways, about level with the kneeling rescuer's chest and roughly 1.5 to 2 metres away. Frame the head, shoulder, elbow, wrist and hip with margin. Film the arm nearest the camera; keep the head and other arm from covering it. Use both hands, tuck elbows in, and hold steady.
4. **Check the scene.** Light the rescuer well; don't put the light behind them, since low light can slow the camera. Use a plain background if possible and keep other people out of frame.
5. **Record a real count.** Ask a third person to count compressions out loud for 30 seconds against a stopwatch. Write down the count and the recording's time.
6. **Finish and report.** After recording, don't uninstall the app or clear anything. Connect the phone by USB and note what the screen showed. Describe results as the current best guess; the next recordings will confirm it. Do not claim accuracy figures.
